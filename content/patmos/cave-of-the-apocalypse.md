## Why go

The Revelation was given here. St John the Theologian, exiled to Patmos
under Domitian, lived in this cave with his disciple Prochoros, and
dictated to him the book that closes the Scriptures. It is the most
historically specific New Testament site anywhere outside the Holy Land —
not a place associated with an event, but the room the event happened in.

## What is there

The cave is small, enclosed now within a chapel built around and into it,
and the things shown are few and are pointed out to every pilgrim:

- **The cleft in the rock of the ceiling**, in three parts, traditionally
  held to have opened when the voice spoke "as of a trumpet";
- **the hollow in the rock where the saint laid his head**;
- **the ledge he used to raise himself**, worn smooth;
- **the niche in which Prochoros wrote**.

Each is marked with silver. The chapel of St Anna is built against the
cave, and the whole is reached down a flight of steps from the road.

## Practical notes

Halfway up the road from Skala to Chora: either a steep 25-minute walk up
the old cobbled footpath — the proper way, and the way pilgrims have always
come — or a five-minute taxi or bus. Combine it with the Monastery of St
John, further up in Chora, in a single morning; both close over the middle
of the day, so a late start wastes the day.

On days when a cruise ship is in Skala the cave is full and the queue long.
Be there at opening.
