## Why go

A fortress monastery founded in 1088 and continuously inhabited since, with
the relics of its founder, a katholikon whose frescoes run from the twelfth
century onwards, and one of the great surviving Byzantine monastic
libraries. The whole of Chora grew up around its walls, and the complex —
church, refectory, chapels, cisterns, cells, treasury — is substantially
medieval.

## What is there to venerate

**The relics of St Christodoulos Latrinos**, the founder, in a side chapel
off the katholikon. He received the island from the Emperor Alexios I
Komnenos by chrysobull in 1088, on condition that it be settled by monks
and no laity or livestock; he died on Euboea in 1093 and his relics were
brought back here.

The katholikon itself, the Chapel of the Theotokos with its twelfth-century
frescoes, and the outer narthex with the cycle of the miracles of St John
are what you have come to see.

## The treasury and library

Separately ticketed and worth it. The treasury holds the original
**chrysobull of Alexios I Komnenos** — the founding charter of the
monastery, with the imperial signature — along with reliquaries, vestments,
icons from the eleventh century on, and church silver. The library is one
of the oldest continuously held monastic collections in the Orthodox world,
with several hundred codices and a few thousand manuscripts, including
leaves of the sixth-century purple Gospel of St Mark.

## History

Patmos was nearly empty when St Christodoulos arrived — the ancient town
abandoned, the island a target for raiders. The monastery was built as a
castle because it had to be, and the island's later history as a
semi-autonomous Orthodox enclave under Ottoman rule, with its own
ecclesiastical school and merchant fleet, flows from its charter.

## Practical notes

Steep and cobbled inside; the last approach is on foot whatever you take up
the hill. Chora's own lanes are worth an hour afterwards — the town is full
of small chapels, most of them locked, and the Panagia Diasozousa and the
other neighbourhood churches are opened by whoever has the key.
