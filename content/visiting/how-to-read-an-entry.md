## The fact block

Every entry opens with the same short block of fields. Each is optional, and
only the ones that are known appear.

**Kind** — monastery, church, cathedral, chapel, cave, holy spring, museum,
ruin, monument, open site, mosque, ancient site. The useful distinction it
draws is between places where there is something to venerate and places
where there is only something to look at.

**To venerate** — what is *physically present and offered for veneration*.
This is deliberately separate from the saints a place is associated with: a
church dedicated to St George does not thereby have St George's relics, and
this field never infers one from the other. If the field is absent, nothing
is recorded as being there — which may mean there is nothing, or may mean it
is not known.

**Feast** — the patronal feast and the feasts of the saints whose relics are
there. Dates are given in the civil calendar; see *Calendars and
jurisdictions* for what that does and does not tell you.

**Jurisdiction** — whose church it is, or that it is a state monument. Stated
as fact, with no judgement attached; see again *Calendars and
jurisdictions*.

**Status** — *active* (a functioning church or monastery), *museum*,
*mosque*, *ruin*, *open site*, or *restricted*.

**Access** — hours, tickets, dress, and whether you are likely to find the
door locked.

**Map** — the coordinates, as an OpenStreetMap link. Every entry with
coordinates also has its own map, and appears as a pin on the overview map on
the front page and on its region's page.

## Ordering

Within a region, places are listed in the order of what a first-time visitor
would give up last, not alphabetically. Below them, under *Also in the area*,
is the alphabetical remainder — places worth going to if you have the day,
but not the reason to make the journey.

## The verification notice

Entries carry a plain-text notice until they have been checked on the ground
or against a printed source. This is not boilerplate caution. Much of what a
gazetteer like this needs — opening hours, which reliquaries are currently
displayed, whether a converted church is open this month — changes faster
than any list can track, and a handful of entries are actively volatile:
anything in Istanbul whose status changed with the Hagia Sophia reconversion
of 2020 or the Chora reconversion of 2024, and any monastery whose hours
contract around its own feasts and fasts.

**Confirm locally before you travel on an entry.** A telephone call to a
monastery gate the week you arrive is worth more than everything written
here.

## Where an entry is a pointer instead of a list

A few entries deliberately say *where to find out* rather than attempting an
answer: the eremitic sites of Patmos are the clearest case. Which cells and
skites can be visited depends on who is living in them, changes, and is not
anyone's to publish. A plausible-looking wrong address is worse than no
address, so those entries name the monastery or the diocese to ask instead.
