## Dress

At monasteries and convents this is enforced at the gate, not merely
requested: long trousers and sleeves for men, a long skirt and sleeves for
women, with a head covering expected in convents. Wraps are often available
at the gate, but not always enough of them to go round when a coach or a boat
has just arrived. **Bring your own and the question never comes up.**

In churches that are now mosques, shoes come off and women cover the head.
Carry a bag for the shoes if a day includes several.

Open sites and museums have no dress code. A functioning church in a town
generally expects no more than ordinary modesty — covered shoulders and
knees.

## Veneration

Things that are easy to bring and impossible to improvise:

- **Small bottles** for holy water, at any shrine with a spring — the
  *ayazma* at Blachernae, the Life-Giving Spring at Balıklı, and the springs
  at monasteries throughout Greece. Bottles are often sold on site, but not
  always, and not always outside the season.
- **Cotton wool** for the myrrh and oil given at reliquaries.
- **A written list of names** — living and departed — for commemoration.
  This is the thing visitors most often wish they had prepared, because
  standing at a reliquary is not the moment to try to remember.
- **Small notes and coins** for candles, bottles, and the occasional ticket.
  Many monasteries take no cards.

## Photography

Forbidden outright inside some shrines — the Cave of the Apocalypse on
Patmos, the Chamber of the Sacred Trusts at Topkapı — and discouraged at
most reliquaries even where it is not posted. **Ask, or do not.** Nothing in
a photograph is worth being the person who had to be told.

## Carrying and footwear

Many of the places in this gazetteer are not reached from a car park.
Hillside sites — Palaiochora, the Areopagus, the Patmos footpath, the
monasteries on Hymettus — are unshaded, and need water and a hat for most of
the year.

Footwear with grip matters more than it sounds. The rock of the Areopagus has
been polished glassy by two thousand years of feet and is genuinely dangerous
in smooth soles; Palaiochora and Kalikatsou are rough broken ground.
