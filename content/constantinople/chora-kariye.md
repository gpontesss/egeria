## Why go

The greatest surviving cycle of late Byzantine mosaic and fresco anywhere —
the Palaiologan renaissance in one building. The narthex mosaics of the
Life of the Theotokos and the Infancy of Christ, the Deësis, and above all
the **Anastasis** in the apse of the funerary chapel, with Christ hauling
Adam and Eve bodily out of their tombs, which is the image the Orthodox
Pascha is painted from.

## History

A monastery church "in the country" (*chora*) outside Constantine's walls,
rebuilt repeatedly, and decorated between about 1315 and 1321 at the
expense of Theodore Metochites, the Grand Logothete, who is shown in the
narthex presenting the church to Christ. Converted to a mosque around 1500,
made a museum in 1945, reconverted in 2024.

## Practical notes

By Edirnekapı, up near the land walls — a taxi from Fener rather than a
walk, and roughly on the axis between the Golden Horn and the western
suburbs. **This is the entry whose status is most likely to have changed
since it was written**; check it before building a day around it.
