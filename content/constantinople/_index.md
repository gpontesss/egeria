Istanbul holds more Orthodox history than anywhere else on earth and very
little functioning Orthodox life: a Patriarchate with a handful of parishes,
a few holy springs, and some thirty Byzantine churches that are now mosques,
museums or ruins. Reading the city means holding both of those at once.

The places fall into natural clusters, which is how to visit them.
**Fener and Balat** on the Golden Horn hold everything that is still
Orthodox — the Patriarchate, St Mary of the Mongols, Blachernae, St Stephen
of the Bulgars — within a walk of each other. **Sultanahmet** holds Hagia
Sophia, Hagia Irene, Topkapı and Sts Sergius and Bacchus in another.
**Chora** and the **Pantokrator** sit between the two, and the **Life-Giving
Spring** at Balıklı is out by the land walls on the airport side.

If a visit is short, the Fener cluster is the one to protect: it is the only
place in the city where there are relics in a functioning Orthodox church
rather than monuments behind a ticket.
