## Why go

The original Zoodochos Pigi — the shrine from which the Bright Friday feast
of the Life-Giving Spring takes its name, and after which every church of
that dedication in the Orthodox world is named, including the convent on
Patmos. A spring in a crypt below the church, with fish in it, and the
legend attached to them: that on the day the City fell a monk frying fish
beside the spring was told the news, said he would believe it when the fish
leapt from the pan, and they leapt back into the water half-fried, where
their descendants are said to swim still. The Turkish name, *Balıklı* —
"with fish" — is from them.

## What is there

The church, the crypt with the spring and the fish, and the adjoining
**patriarchal cemetery**, where many Ecumenical Patriarchs of the modern
period are buried — a quiet and little-visited place that is arguably more
affecting than the shrine itself.

## Practical notes

Outside the land walls to the west, near the Silivri Gate, which puts it on
the opposite side of the city from Sultanahmet — fifteen to twenty-five
minutes by taxi, and closer to the western approaches than to the monument
district. Easiest to take at the beginning or the end of a day rather than
in the middle of one.
