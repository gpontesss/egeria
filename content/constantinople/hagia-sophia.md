## Why go

The Great Church — the cathedral of the Christian East for nine centuries,
the building in which the Byzantine rite took the shape it still has, and
where the legates deposited the bull of excommunication on the altar in
1054. Justinian's church of 537, and still, as architecture, without a
peer.

## What is there

The **Deësis** mosaic in the south gallery — Christ with the Theotokos and
the Forerunner, thirteenth century, and the finest Byzantine mosaic
anywhere — along with the imperial portrait panels (Constantine IX and
Zoe; John II Komnenos and Irene), the Virgin and Child in the apse, the
seraphim in the pendentives, and the marble revetment and the
*omphalion* where emperors were crowned. The mosaics in the galleries
survived because they were plastered rather than destroyed.

Since the 2020 reconversion the figural mosaics at ground level are
curtained during prayers, and access arrangements have changed more than
once. **This is not a place of Orthodox veneration** — there is nothing here
to venerate and no Orthodox service. It is a visit to a lost church, which is
its own thing.

## Practical notes

Hagia Irene (in the first courtyard of Topkapı, below), the Church of
Sts Sergius and Bacchus, the Basilica Cistern, the Column of Constantine
and the Hippodrome are all within a short walk, so Sultanahmet is one
compact half-day. The queue and the prayer-time closures are the two things
that eat a schedule — check the day's prayer times before committing to an
hour.
