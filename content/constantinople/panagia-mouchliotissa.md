## Why go

**The only Byzantine church in Constantinople that has been in continuous
Orthodox use since before 1453 and was never converted into a mosque.** It
was granted that status by firman of Mehmed II, reportedly out of regard
for the Greek architect of the Fatih Mosque, and the Patriarchate has kept
the decree. The Turks call it *Kanlı Kilise*, the bloody church, from the
fighting on this hill during the conquest.

Its name comes from Maria Palaiologina, a daughter of Michael VIII, who was
married to a Mongol Ilkhan, returned to the City as a widow, became a nun,
and restored the church and the convent attached to it in the late
thirteenth century.

## Practical notes

Up the steep lane from the Patriarchate towards the red-brick bulk of the
Phanar Greek College — five minutes' walk, and the three Fener sites
(Patriarchate, Mouchliotissa, Blachernae) are naturally one visit. Finding it
open is the difficulty; ask at the Patriarchate gate.
