## Why go

The single most important stop in the city, and the one to protect if the
day runs short. The seat of the Ecumenical Patriarchate since 1601
holds the greatest concentration of relics in the city, and unlike almost
everything else in Constantinople it is a functioning Orthodox church, not
a monument.

## What is there to venerate

Along the right-hand side of the nave, in glass-fronted reliquaries open
for veneration:

- **St Gregory the Theologian** and **St John Chrysostom** — their relics
  were carried off from Constantinople by the Fourth Crusade in 1204 and
  kept in Rome for eight centuries; they were returned by Pope John Paul II
  to Patriarch Bartholomew in November 2004 and have been here since.
- **St Basil the Great** — a portion of his relics.
- **St Euphemia of Chalcedon**, the great martyr whose relics are
  associated with the vindication of the Fourth Ecumenical Council;
  **St Theophano**, the first wife of the Emperor Leo VI; and
  **St Solomone**, mother of the Maccabean martyrs.

Also shown: a **portion of the column of the Flagellation**, brought from
Jerusalem; the patriarchal throne, traditionally said to go back to
St John Chrysostom; and a mosaic-rich iconostasis.

Verify the present composition of the reliquaries on the spot rather than
from this page — relics are occasionally moved, or lent out for feasts.

## The gate

The main gate of the patriarchal compound has been **welded shut since 1821**
and has never been reopened: Patriarch Gregory V was hanged from it on
Pascha of that year. You enter by the side gate. The sealed gate itself is
one of the things to see, and the saint who hung on it is venerated in
Athens (see *Athens → Metropolitan Cathedral*).

## History

The Patriarchate moved here in 1601, after the loss of Hagia Sophia in 1453
and a series of intermediate seats. The church is modest in scale and
largely nineteenth-century in its present form after fires — this is a
church of relics and of continuity, not of architecture.

## Practical notes

Fener is on the Golden Horn, well up from Sultanahmet, and is not walkable
from the main monument district — 20–40 minutes by taxi depending on
traffic. Pair it with the Holy Spring of Blachernae and the Church of St
Mary of the Mongols, both close by, in a single Fener–Balat loop. That loop
is the best two hours an Orthodox visitor can spend in Istanbul.
