## Why go

Of all the lost holy places of Constantinople this is the one that most
shaped the Orthodox liturgical year. The great church of the Theotokos at
Blachernae held the **Robe of the Theotokos**, brought from Palestine in
the fifth century; it was here that the all-night vigil of 626 was kept
during the Avar siege and the **Akathist Hymn** — "To thee, the Champion
Leader" — was first sung in thanksgiving; and here that St Andrew the Fool
for Christ saw the Theotokos spread her veil over the congregation, the
vision commemorated as the **Protection (Pokrov / Αγία Σκέπη)**.

The Byzantine church burned in 1434 and is gone. The holy spring remained,
and a small modern church stands over it.

## What is there

A small walled courtyard, a modest church, and the **ayazma** — the holy
spring — in a marble basin below, from which pilgrims draw water. It is
unglamorous and easy to miss, and it is the site of the Akathist.

## Practical notes

In the Fener–Balat quarter, roughly a ten-minute walk or a short taxi from
the Patriarchate — the two belong on one loop, together with St Mary of the
Mongols. Bring a bottle for the water.
