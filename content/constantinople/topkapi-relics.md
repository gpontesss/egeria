## Why go

Among the Islamic relics in the Chamber of the Sacred Trusts the Ottomans
also kept Christian ones gathered from the conquered City, and two are on
display: **an arm and a portion of the skull attributed to St John the
Forerunner**, in Ottoman reliquary casings — the arm encased in a chased
gold sleeve with a window over the hand. Also shown are objects attributed
to Moses, Abraham, Joseph and David.

This is a museum case, not a shrine, and there is no veneration — but it is
the only place in the city where relics taken from Byzantine
Constantinople can still be seen in it.

## Practical notes

Topkapı is large and slow; if the Sacred Trusts are the only reason for
going, budget an hour and a half anyway for the ticket, the walk through the
courtyards and the queue. Hagia Irene stands in the first courtyard,
inside the palace walls, which makes the two one stop.
