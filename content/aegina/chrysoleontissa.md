## Why go

The principal historic monastery of Aegina and the island's great
Marian pilgrimage, built as a fortified house in the seventeenth century
against pirate raids — high walls, a tower, a courtyard garden. Its
miraculous icon of the Theotokos, the Chrysoleontissa, is the object of the
pilgrimage, and the reason the islanders themselves come here rather than to
St Nektarios.

The carved and gilded iconostasis and the monastery's collection of icons
and manuscripts are worth the trip on their own.

## Practical notes

Needs a car or taxi; there is little public transport this way. The Temple
of Aphaia is on the same side of the island, for anyone who wants the
classical half of Aegina too.
