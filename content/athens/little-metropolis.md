## Why go

The most remarkable small church in Athens, and the clearest single object
lesson in how Christian Athens was built out of pagan Athens. Its exterior
is a mosaic of reused ancient marble — a complete ancient calendar frieze of
the Attic festival year runs around it, with crosses cut into the pagan
reliefs by whoever assembled the wall.

## History

Twelfth century, built almost entirely of *spolia*: classical and Early
Christian blocks, inscriptions and reliefs taken from older buildings and
fitted together. It served as the metropolitan church of Athens in the
Ottoman period, which is where the name comes from — it is "little" by
contrast with the nineteenth-century cathedral now beside it. Dedicated
both to the Theotokos *Gorgoepikoos* ("she who is quick to hear") and to St
Eleftherios.

## Practical notes

Walk the outside wall slowly before going in; the building's interest is
largely on its skin. The interior is tiny and dim.
