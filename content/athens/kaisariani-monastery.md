## Why go

An eleventh-century katholikon in a walled compound of cells, refectory,
bath-house and mill, set in cypress on the side of Hymettus — one of the
most complete surviving Middle Byzantine monastic complexes in Attica, and
the most atmospheric place within an hour of Syntagma. The church's
frescoes are seventeenth-century, by a Peloponnesian painter, and intact.

## History

Founded in the eleventh century on a site with a spring sacred since
antiquity; the monastery was wealthy and independent through the Frankish
and Ottoman periods and was dissolved in the nineteenth century. It is now
maintained as a monument.

## Practical notes

No Liturgy, no relics, no veneration — this is a museum visit and should be
planned as one. It pairs with the walk up to the Monastery of St John the
Hunter, higher on the same mountain. Reachable by bus plus a 1.5 km uphill
walk, or by taxi.
