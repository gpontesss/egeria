## Why go

The finest Byzantine mosaics in Attica and among the finest anywhere: the
Pantokrator in the dome, the Nativity, the Baptism, the Dormition, in the
restrained classicising style of the late eleventh century. If you care
about Byzantine art as such, this is the single most important building on
the Athens list.

## History

Built around 1080 on the site of a sanctuary of Apollo on the Sacred Way to
Eleusis — the name is from the laurels (*daphnai*) sacred to Apollo. Taken
over by Cistercians under Frankish rule in the thirteenth century, returned
to Orthodox monks in the Ottoman period, dissolved in the nineteenth
century. Badly damaged in the 1999 Athens earthquake and under restoration
for many years afterwards.

## Practical notes

A museum, not a church — no services, no relics. Its opening hours have
been irregular and narrow for years; this is the one Attica site most worth
confirming by telephone before setting out, because a wasted journey costs
half a day.
