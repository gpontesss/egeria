## Why go

The emblematic Byzantine church of Athens, standing alone in the middle of
the city's main shopping street with the traffic routed around it — it was
saved from demolition in the 1830s by the personal intervention of
Ludwig I of Bavaria. Eleventh century, cross-in-square with a dome on four
columns, with a later narthex and side chapel. The interior mosaics and
frescoes are twentieth-century work by Photis Kontoglou, which is itself a
reason to go: Kontoglou is the painter who turned Greek church art back
towards the Byzantine tradition.

## Practical notes

On the way between Syntagma and Monastiraki, and costs nothing to add to a
day.
